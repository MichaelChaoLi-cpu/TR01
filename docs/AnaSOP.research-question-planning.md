# AnaSOP
Analysis Standard Operating Procedure

## 1. Research Objective

### Central Research Question

- Research type: applied
- Research question: Under a common constrained pavement-maintenance budget in Japan, how do traffic-volume, pavement-condition and basic-service-accessibility priorities differ in the accessibility losses they avoid and in the distribution of protection across residents, particularly in ageing areas? Bridge maintenance is an independent supplementary comparison.
- Why it matters: Maintenance decisions allocate scarce resources across roads whose traffic demand and contribution to essential-service access may differ. A transparent comparison can inform which residents and places are protected as budgets contract.
- Data support currently visible: Nationwide population and age supports, medical destinations, candidate road connectivity, road-dependence outcomes and a bounded measured-traffic correspondence have been constructed. Official spatial population projections support conditional demographic scenarios. The complete public pavement basic inventory has been acquired and audited: 472,720 unique records across 47 prefectures, including 454,724 with usable observed grades. Its final road correspondence and eligible policy sample remain unverified. The completed bridge comparison has separate observed inspection inputs and explicitly assumed costs and effects; these do not establish pavement inputs.
- Key readable constructs: Pavement Maintenance Budget; Pavement Maintenance Priority; Avoided Accessibility Loss; Essential-Service Network Access; Population; Ageing Composition. The pavement scenario adopts segment-length costs and finite simultaneous impedance effects, while final anchored road inputs and readable variable construction remain pending. Existing bridge-variable definitions apply only to the independent supplement.
- What would verify it: Compare the protection delivered by the three priorities within a common pavement decision domain, using comparable costs, budget and explicit deterioration or service-loss assumptions. Report disconnected origins, age-location uncertainty and observed input coverage. Evaluate the bridge supplement within its own domain and budget denominator.
- What would falsify or weaken the proposed policy contrast: Indistinguishable protection distributions across rules, an advantage that disappears under defensible input assumptions, or inadequate coverage of condition, traffic and intervention effects. The question does not presume that accessibility-based allocation is always preferable.
- Required next feasibility check: Establish pavement-specific inspection coverage and road correspondence, practical maintenance units, comparable costs and a defensible maintenance-to-service relationship. Preserve the nationwide source domain while reporting the actual policy sample. If hypothetical inputs are needed, obtain explicit research decisions and label the comparison conditional scenario analysis.

### Supporting Research Questions

#### Supporting Point 1: Baseline service-access disadvantage

- Research question: Where is basic-service network access poor, and how does its spatial distribution relate to ageing composition and population concentration?
- Why it matters: Allocation outcomes must be judged against the residents and locations facing access disadvantage before any simulated intervention.
- Data support currently visible: Population meshes, age publication groups, medical and fire-facility locations, and nationwide candidate road-distance diagnostics are available.
- Key readable constructs: Medical Network Distance; Fire-Station Network Distance; Network Reachability; Population; Ageing Composition; Population Concentration.
- What would verify it: Describe service-specific access and demographic patterns on compatible spatial supports, retaining unresolved road-entry and unreachable records rather than assigning them a zero distance.
- What would falsify or weaken the proposed pattern: No material relationship between ageing composition and disadvantage, or spatial contrasts dominated by publication aggregation, facility-date mismatches or road-entry assumptions.
- Required next feasibility check: Characterize remaining operating-status and candidate-network coverage limitations. The accepted medical baseline now uses registered hospitals and general clinics, local-total-population-weighted finite-path distances and separate path-status shares on official age supports. The main ageing measure uses the confirmed known-age denominator defined in Section 4; older residents are not located within member meshes.

#### Supporting Point 2: Dependence on roads with limited alternatives

- Research question: Which road sections or maintenance assets protect essential-service access that nearby alternative routes cannot adequately replace, and how does that role compare with their measured traffic demand?
- Why it matters: A road with modest observed traffic can still be important for residents who have few alternative connections to essential destinations. This is a proposition to test, not an established feature of all low-volume roads.
- Data support currently visible: Nationwide single-source-road service-loss outcomes have been computed and validated. The measured-traffic comparison has a bounded, validated correspondence. These constructions describe road dependence; they do not establish pavement deterioration or maintenance benefits.
- Key readable constructs: Road or Asset Service Dependence; Alternative-Route Distance; Reachability Loss; Affected Population; Observed Traffic Volume.
- What would verify it: Evaluate changes in access under explicit, plausible service-loss scenarios and compare the affected populations and route alternatives with measured traffic where linkage is reliable.
- What would falsify or weaken the proposed contrast: Adequate alternatives eliminate material losses, dependence follows traffic closely, or identified criticality is primarily a graph-construction artifact.
- Required next feasibility check: Determine how pavement inspection intervals correspond to the candidate network and whether the eventual pavement service-loss model can use the completed road-dependence diagnostics. Complete road removal is an adopted dependence scenario, not an automatic consequence of deferred pavement maintenance.

#### Supporting Point 3: Distributional trade-offs under a shared budget

- Research question: How much aggregate accessibility protection, and protection for initially disadvantaged or ageing areas, does each prioritization rule deliver at comparable budget levels?
- Why it matters: A policy comparison should reveal who receives protection and what aggregate protection is gained or sacrificed, rather than rely solely on a single inequality statistic.
- Data support currently visible: Demographic, accessibility and area-group constructions are available. Three bridge priorities have been evaluated in a bounded common domain. Public pavement records have been acquired; the main comparison remains gated by conditional road correspondence, repeated-grade aggregation, final variable construction and priority algorithms under the adopted cost and impairment assumptions.
- Key readable constructs: Budget; Avoided Accessibility Loss; Population-Weighted Protection; Protection of Initially Disadvantaged Areas; Ageing-Area Protection.
- What would verify it: Evaluate all pavement rules using a common pavement sample, costs and scenarios, reporting aggregate protection together with its distribution and unresolved population coverage. Keep bridge protection and spending accounting separate.
- What would falsify or weaken the proposed policy difference: Protection is similar across groups, a rule improves distribution only by reducing protection broadly, or the ranking depends on incomparable budgets or unsupported monetary weights.
- Required next feasibility check: Verify cost and intervention-effect comparability and select a defensible disadvantage definition and demographic support. Do not equate maintenance spending with an observed improvement without an intervention model.

#### Supporting Point 4: Budget contraction and demographic change

- Research question: Under explicitly defined budget-contraction and demographic-change scenarios, how stable are maintenance priorities and their distributional consequences?
- Why it matters: Present population and traffic may not represent future maintenance needs where the resident population shrinks or ages, but these changes must be measured or stated as assumptions.
- Data support currently visible: A 2020 census baseline and official spatial population projections have been harmonized for a bounded bridge sensitivity comparison, retaining a matched reference and fixed area groups. Future population weights may be reusable after the pavement framework is confirmed. No observed fiscal trajectory or longitudinal causal depopulation design is available.
- Key readable constructs: Budget Scenario; Population-Change Scenario; Ageing Scenario; Priority Stability; Distribution of Protected Access.
- What would verify it: Compare policies over transparent budget and demographic scenarios using compatible demographic supports, while separating projections and assumptions from observed changes.
- What would falsify or weaken the proposed sensitivity: Priorities remain largely stable, scenario results have no material policy consequence, or arbitrary demographic allocations determine the apparent effect.
- Required next feasibility check: Confirm the pavement intervention domain and outcomes before extending adopted projection-based weights to pavement scenarios. Future weights do not locate future older residents or forecast traffic; relative budget scenarios do not establish observed fiscal contraction.

### Scope of Analysis

- Geographic scope: Nationwide Japan as the source domain. The main baseline analysis excludes meshes without a calculable medical network distance and all members of official age supports without a calculable ageing share. Excluded source records remain in the baseline archive and sample accounting. Findings concern the selected baseline population, not unconditional coverage of all residents of Japan; additional policy inputs may further constrain coverage.
- Demographic reference: Census population in 2020, with age counts represented on their official publication supports. Road reference is September 2024. Facilities have differing historical reference years. The current configuration is not a synchronized observed network year.
- Units: Population meshes for local total population and candidate origin distances; official publication groups for age counts; existing candidate-network road segments for main pavement maintenance. The policy domain requires measured traffic, usable official pavement inspection categories and an explicit conditional anchor rule, with missing inputs withheld. Exact anchor and aggregation definitions and eligible coverage remain to be constructed. The independent bridge supplement retains its 46,688 conditional one-bridge/one-source-road units.
- Essential services: Medical and emergency-service access provide the current diagnostic starting point. Welfare and other acquired facility types remain candidate destinations whose roles must be confirmed; acquisition alone does not select an analysis outcome.
- Population decline and economic context: Budget scarcity belongs to the policy problem. Official population projections support conditional spatial reweighting, not observed population decline. Local economic conditions and municipal fiscal capacity require additional data before entering an empirical specification.
- Ageing interpretation: Areas with an older age composition are not equivalent to observed elderly travelers, individual mobility impairment or actual service users. The main ageing denominator is known-age population on the official publication support, as defined in Section 4. The accepted group-level medical-access context uses member-mesh total-population weights and separate path-status shares; individual older residents are not located within groups.

### Study Design Declaration

- Applied spatial policy evaluation with pavement maintenance as the main policy analysis and bridge maintenance as an independent supplement. Existing baseline and road-dependence diagnostics remain reusable. Pavement and bridge interventions do not share a joint allocation budget.
- No causal effect of ageing, depopulation or maintenance is identified by the current cross-sectional data. Simulated avoided loss is a model-dependent policy outcome, not an observed treatment effect.
- Road distance is the current diagnostic metric. Travel time requires usable speed and vehicle-routing rules. Fire-station network proximity under the user-specified ambulance-base assumption does not measure dispatch delay, vehicle availability, patient outcomes or hospital transport time.
- The user permits explicit, documented assumptions when acquiring a required pavement input is impractical. Prefer available observations, identify assumed inputs and keep resulting policy claims conditional. This permission does not determine a specific unit, cost or service-effect parameter.
- Adopted pavement assumptions: relative cost is proportional to road-segment length; unmaintained eligible segments simultaneously receive a uniform 25 percent impedance increase, with 10 and 50 percent sensitivity settings; maintenance restores baseline impedance. Other network segments remain at baseline. Requested budgets are 1, 5, 10 and 20 percent of total pavement-domain relative cost. These are conditional service-cost scenarios, not measured speed changes, failure probabilities or expenditure. The bridge supplement retains its independent area-cost, full-prevention and equally weighted event framework.

## 2. Theoretical Background  /  Conceptual Framework  /  Problem Formulation

### Research Gap

The applied policy problem is how to protect essential-service access when maintenance resources are constrained and the population served varies in age composition and spatial concentration. Traffic demand, measured asset need and dependence on a particular service connection can lead to different maintenance priorities.

The proposed main contribution is a transparent comparison of pavement-maintenance priorities within the verified Japan-wide source domain and its eligible policy sample, linking protected road function to population supports and essential services. The existing conditional bridge comparison provides independent supplementary evidence, rather than a proxy for all-road pavement maintenance. Existing research has already incorporated social equity into maintenance programming and budget allocation. Novelty therefore requires a focused literature review and evidence that the demographic and service-dependence formulation adds policy insight; neither nationwide scope nor inclusion of an equity statistic alone establishes originality.

### Conceptual Framework

Population location and age composition determine who may depend on road-connected services. Network configuration and alternative destinations determine how service access changes when a road's usable function is reduced. Traffic and physical condition provide different indicators of demand and asset need. A maintenance policy chooses interventions within a common resource constraint, and each intervention must have a defensible effect on usable road function before its accessibility protection can be evaluated.

The conceptual sequence is: demographic and service context; dependence on road connections; explicit deterioration or service-loss assumptions; budget-constrained maintenance priorities; avoided accessibility losses and their distribution. This sequence describes the policy comparison and its assumptions, not a causal relationship established by the available data.

Efficiency concerns aggregate protection of access. Equity concerns the distribution of that protection and remaining disadvantage across population supports. Protection of essential-service access for initially disadvantaged areas is a candidate normative objective, not proof that one policy dominates on every criterion. Differences in average access, access floors and demographic distribution should be made visible rather than collapsed prematurely into an unexamined composite score.

### Problem Formulation

Compare traffic-volume, pavement-condition and basic-service-accessibility priorities within the same pavement allocation budget, decision units, cost accounting and service-loss scenarios. Analyze bridges independently using their retained common sample and budget denominator; no joint pavement-bridge spending comparison is adopted. Outcomes concern the access loss avoided by protecting the selected road function, together with the distribution of that protection. A present-day nearest-facility map by itself does not specify a maintenance benefit.

Maintain separate spatial supports for local population, officially aggregated age counts and network origins. A recipient group's age count cannot be treated as the recipient mesh's elderly count or assigned exclusively to its road entrance. Any group-to-accessibility linkage must preserve the observed count support and state the additional location assumptions or uncertainty.

The main pavement comparison adopts finite impedance growth on unmaintained eligible road segments and restoration to baseline on maintained segments. All selected and unselected segments are evaluated together on the full network, allowing routes and nearest destinations to change. Pavement inspection grades inform maintenance need; the adopted uniform impedance increases are explicit scenario assumptions and are not calibrated from those grades. Structural bridge grades cannot substitute for pavement inspection labels. The existing bridge/tunnel and road-state attributes are geometric or source-state descriptors; they do not by themselves establish pavement condition, defect severity, deterioration probability or repair cost.

The empirical prerequisites are representative origin access, eligible destinations, a defensible road/asset intervention unit, traffic and condition observations, comparable intervention costs, and an explicit intervention-effect relationship. If some inputs remain unavailable, the permitted result is a clearly conditional policy scenario or a narrower descriptive study. It must not be relabeled as an observed national maintenance program or evidence of causal ageing/depopulation effects.
